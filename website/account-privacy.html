Account and billing privacy.

How your Saiph account, sign-in and Saiph Cloud subscription data are handled. Last updated 16 October 2026.

Who is responsible

Alexander Belik, a sole trader in Ireland trading as Abelik1, is the controller for Saiph accounts and billing. Contact support@usesaiph.com; the postal address is on the contact page. The website notice covers the public web pages separately.

Sign-in and essential cookies

Auth0 (Okta) handles sign-in. It receives your email address and profile, login details, and network, device and sign-in event information. If you sign in with Google, Google also processes that login under its own privacy policy.

The account site keeps only your account identifier in its session. It uses a secure, HTTP-only, SameSite Lax session cookie that ends after one hour (or 30 minutes of inactivity), plus temporary cookies during sign-in. Auth0 keeps its own sign-in session on its domain. These cookies are needed to sign you in; none are used for advertising or analytics.

Legal basis: providing the account you asked for, and our legitimate interest in protecting accounts against abuse.

Subscriptions and payments

Stripe collects your card and billing details on its own pages; we never receive full card numbers or security codes. We store your account identifier and the Stripe customer, subscription, price and charge identifiers, subscription status and dates, payment-failure and dispute status, withdrawal and refund status, and a short record of each Stripe event so it is processed once. To give you access, the Saiph Cloud service receives your account identifier, a subscription reference and the date until which access is paid.

Stripe acts as our processor for payments and also as an independent controller for its own regulatory and fraud-prevention purposes, described in Stripe's privacy policy.

Legal bases: performing your subscription contract, keeping records the law requires, and our legitimate interest in preventing fraud and handling disputes.

Who processes your data, and where

  • Auth0 (Okta), sign-in: United States.
  • Vercel, hosts the account site: Dublin, Ireland, served through Vercel's global network.
  • Neon, account and billing database: London, United Kingdom.
  • DigitalOcean, runs the Saiph Cloud service: London, United Kingdom.
  • Stripe, payments: Stripe Payments Europe, Ireland, with processing elsewhere as Stripe describes.
  • ImprovMX and Google (Gmail), support email: forwarded and stored in their systems, including in the United States.

The United Kingdom is covered by an EU adequacy decision. Transfers to the United States rely on the providers' EU-US Data Privacy Framework certification or the standard contractual clauses in their data processing terms. People who help us, such as an accountant or legal adviser, and authorities where the law requires it, may also receive information. Provider policies: Okta, Vercel, Neon, DigitalOcean, Stripe, ImprovMX and Google.

How long we keep it

  • Account and profile data: until you ask us to delete your account, then removed within 30 days.
  • Billing and payment records: six years after the end of the tax year they relate to, as Irish tax law requires.
  • Support emails: 12 months after the matter is resolved, unless needed longer for a dispute or billing record.
  • Technical and security logs: no longer than needed for security and troubleshooting, normally up to 30 days.

To delete your account, cancel any subscription first, then email us; there is no self-service deletion yet. Deleting your account does not cancel a subscription, and records we must keep by law are kept only for that purpose. Providers delete their copies under their own policies.

Your content is a separate matter

The account site never receives your clipboard, messages or files, and paying or signing in does not approve devices or unlock content. With Saiph Cloud, your devices encrypt content before it leaves them. The service can still see your account, device public keys, connection times and message sizes. See the Cloud page for details.

Phone and desktop app privacy

You choose which approved devices can connect. Signing in alone does not approve a device. Saiph stores device keys and approval history on your devices; removing a phone from the app keeps that protected history for recovery. Remove its approval on your main computer as well.

Texts and calls are off until you enable them on the phone and grant Android access. When enabled, approved computers on your account can receive text messages, numbers, contact names and call state, and can send texts, dial, answer or decline calls. Cloud devices encrypt this content before sending it through the service. The service still sees routing information, public device keys, connection times and encrypted message sizes. This describes the design; beta release checks and independent security review are listed in the release status.

Pictures in text messages (MMS) and group replies stay on the phone. Call audio stays on the phone. If two computers answer or decline the same call, the first accepted action wins and the other computer is notified. These are feature limits, not additional permissions.

Notification and clipboard sharing use the access you allow in the app and Android Settings. Files you choose to send are shared with the approved destination. Your own Hub is operated by you or its owner, whose handling of service data you should review separately. Local weekly summaries stay on the computer; turning them off clears their saved counts. Usage statistics require the separate choice below.

Photo gallery sharing is off until you choose Allow photos on your phone and grant Android photo access. It shares previews of up to 100 recent accessible photos with your approved computers; Android can limit this to photos you select. Originals transfer when you open, copy or save them, with a 20 MB transfer limit. Cloud previews, filenames and originals are encrypted between devices. Stop sharing photos to stop new transfers and remove synced gallery previews as computers reconnect; copies you already saved remain on those computers.

Optional app usage statistics

Saiph's desktop and Android apps ask once whether to share anonymous daily usage counts; nothing is counted or sent unless you agree, and you can turn it off in Settings, which deletes the counts kept on that device. A batch says which features and screens were used that day in rough ranges, the app version and platform, how the device connects and how far setup progressed. It contains no content, contacts, phone numbers, names, app or file names, account, device or install identifier, so batches cannot be linked to you or to each other. The Saiph service adds each batch to daily totals and keeps neither the batch nor your network address; totals are deleted after 400 days. The apps show every batch before it is sent. The full field list is published in the source repository (docs/USAGE_ANALYTICS.md). The legal basis is your consent, which you can withdraw at any time.

Your choices and rights

You can ask for access to, correction, deletion, restriction or a portable copy of your data, object to processing based on legitimate interests, and withdraw any consent at any time without affecting earlier processing. Account and billing data are never added to launch-update emails without your separate consent, and there is no advertising or analytics on the account site.

Email support@usesaiph.com. We answer within one month; if we need longer, we will tell you why within that month. You can complain to the Irish Data Protection Commission or your local supervisory authority. Access to the account is decided by automated checks of your sign-in; we do no credit or eligibility profiling. Stripe's own fraud decisions are described in its notice.
